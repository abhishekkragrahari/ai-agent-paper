## 1. What each prior paper already solves (verified, with confidence level)

| Paper | Solves | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **EBFT** (He & Yu, arXiv:2607.16109) | Defines *epistemic fault* (protocol-compliant, non-equivocating, semantically invalid endorsement). Formalizes EBFT with two independent confidence-indexed bounds: e_δ (coherent invalid-endorsement rate outside the Byzantine set) and u_ε (unusable-support / liveness-degradation rate). Derives quorum-threshold conditions for semantic validity, agreement, and liveness as three **separated** properties, and states a calibration methodology for estimating e_δ/u_ε. Explicitly states agreement alone does not guarantee semantic validity. Names the correlation problem (shared weights/training distributions/prompts/toolchains) as the reason epistemic faults are correlated, but treats correlation as an *input* to the bound, not something it decomposes by source. | High — multiple independent search passes converge on identical technical details (the two-bound structure, the "left to endpoint execution"-style framing echoed in the companion paper). |
| **EFD / DAQC** (He & Yu, arXiv:2609.02925) | Formalizes correlated failure via an explicit, modeled **Epistemic Fault Basis** — named instantiations include endpoint, service, upstream-data, availability-zone, and **provider** bases (provider basis is explicitly described as covering "model weights or lineage, training distributions" sharing). Defines Epistemic Fault Domain (EFD), Structural Epistemic Cut κ_E (minimum fault roots whose activation compromises a decisive coalition), and Dependency-Aware Quorum Controller (DAQC), which enforces cuts at admission time. Ships a frozen, **reported** 120-task benchmark (5 categories × 16 unsafe/8 safe) whose evidence packages are rooted in **upstream-data/evidence-root** sources (a CloudWatch metric, a VPC flow-log analyzer, a DB transaction-status check) — i.e., the benchmark that is actually run instantiates the evidence-root basis. The paper states, in its own words (as surfaced by search), that *"whether actual models propagate that [modeled] fault into unsafe approvals is an empirical question left to endpoint execution."* | High on the definitions and benchmark composition (converged across 3 independent search passes with consistent numeric/structural detail). **Medium** on whether a provider-basis experiment exists elsewhere in the paper — no search pass surfaced one, but full-text absence cannot be confirmed (see §0). |
| **H-CSC** (Xu, Zhang, Ounis, Wang, arXiv:2606.07316) | A BFT-inspired protocol (Hierarchical Certified Semantic Commitment) converting embedding-derived finality signals over verdict-conditioned proposals into a typed outcome: `semantic_commit`, `verdict_commit`, or a typed abort. Evaluated on two real benchmarks: BCS_v1 (120 episodes, controlled semantic-poisoning) and **MVR-50** (50 real LLM-agent claim-verification tasks) under paired static and rushing Byzantine attacks, reporting commit rates 0.90/0.92 and honest-reference-invalid rates 0.02/0.00. Compares against `majority_vote` and `confidence_weighted_vote` baselines, and a "certificate-wrapped majority" baseline (B3) that H-CSC's headline numbers are reported as numerically close to — H-CSC's distinguishing contribution is *typed semantic provenance* (the embedding-backed digest, emitted on 74%/72% of rounds), not raw safety/coverage improvement over that baseline. | High — numeric results (0.90/0.92, 0.02/0.00, 74%/72%, BCS_v1's 0.31–2.04° angular deviation) are specific enough that they are very unlikely to be search-engine hallucination; they recur identically across independent query phrasings. |
| **SSVP / Context Drift** (Rodrigues, arXiv:2606.21666) | Defines Context Divergence Score (CDS) and Shared State Verification Protocol (SSVP); treats hallucination in concurrent multi-agent settings as *state staleness* between agents rather than a quorum-safety problem. Evaluated in two domains (travel planning, software project planning): naive full-broadcast synchronization *increases* hallucination 34% over a no-sync baseline; SSVP avoids that regression while cutting API calls 58% relative to full broadcast. | High — the specific, counterintuitive 34%-increase number recurs identically across passes. |
| **Correlated Errors in LLMs** (Kim et al., ICML 2025 / arXiv:2506.07962) | Empirically demonstrates, across 350+ models on two leaderboards plus a resume-screening task, that LLM errors correlate substantially (60% co-error agreement on one leaderboard) and that this correlation persists across distinct architectures/providers once models are large/accurate — attributed to shared training corpora/objectives ("algorithmic monoculture"), not shared runtime infrastructure. No quorum/consensus/admission-control framing anywhere in the surfaced material. | High. |
| **Consensus is Not Verification** (arXiv:2603.06612) | Empirically shows, across 5 benchmarks (factual knowledge, commonsense, expert-level QA, forecasting) and multiple open-source models, that polling/self-consistency aggregation gives no consistent accuracy gain over single-sample baselines even at 25× inference cost, and *amplifies* shared misconceptions. Shows correlation surviving even on out-of-distribution random-string generation — a condition with **zero shared runtime dependency**. No quorum/consensus-protocol or admission-control framing; this is an ML-evaluation paper, not a systems paper. Exact model list (which specific commercial/open models) was **not** surfaced by search — flagged as unverified. | Medium-high on the qualitative findings (converged across 2 passes); **low** on exact model/benchmark names, which must be confirmed from primary text before citing them by name in a manuscript. |

## 2. What none of them solves — the residual gap, precisely stated

No paper among the six, as verified:

1. **Reports an experiment that varies the *provider basis* with real, differently-trained LLM agents inside a quorum/admission-control framing.** DAQC's own reported benchmark instantiates only the evidence-root basis; no search pass surfaced a provider-basis run, table, or figure in DAQC, and DAQC's own text flags the real-model behavioral question for this class as open ("left to endpoint execution"). H-CSC's baselines are majority/confidence-weighted voting and its own protocol — not a structural-vs-provider factorial design. Neither EBFT nor SSVP touches this axis at all.
2. **Connects Kim et al.'s / arXiv:2603.06612's empirically-measured cross-provider correlation magnitude to a quorum admission-control mechanism (κ_E or otherwise).** Both are ML-evaluation papers with no admission-control framing; neither computes or references κ_E, EFD, or any DAQC-style structural cut.
3. **Measures observed, continuous semantic-error correlation (e.g., a correlation coefficient, co-error probability, or mutual-information estimate between specific agent pairs) and tests whether it — rather than a coarse provider/model-family label — is the better predictor of quorum-level false consensus.** This specific ablation (measured correlation vs. label) does not appear in any of the six as verified.

## 3. What our paper contributes beyond the prior six (final form, post-correction)

> An empirical test, using a measured (not label-based) semantic-error-correlation framework, of whether DAQC's own evidence-root κ_E-safety criterion — the basis type its reported benchmark actually exercises — leaves a residual, non-zero correlation-driven false-consensus risk once enforced; and whether that residual correlation is better predicted by continuous pairwise correlation measures (§ `CORRELATION_MEASUREMENT.md`) than by the coarse provider-label proxy used informally in the correlated-errors ML literature.

This is explicitly: (a) a test of a question DAQC's own paper states is open for the basis type it names but (as verified) does not report running; (b) the first joint use of Kim et al./#6-style correlation measurement inside a κ_E-style admission-control framing; (c) an ablation (measured correlation vs. provider label as predictor) that is novel relative to all six as verified, because none of the six computes both quantities and compares them.

## 4. Is the contribution still genuinely differentiated? Honest verdict.

**Conditionally yes, with one explicit, load-bearing caveat**: this verdict rests on the absence of a provider-basis / cross-model-family experiment in DAQC and H-CSC's full text, which could not be confirmed by full-text read in this environment (§0). If such an experiment exists and is discovered later (by the user, a reviewer, or a future session with arXiv access), item (1) in §3 must be withdrawn and the contribution narrows to items (2)–(3) alone — which, on their own, are still a real, citable, non-trivial methodological contribution (nobody has run the correlation-measurement-vs-label ablation described in item (3)), but a reviewer aware of the missing experiment would be right to describe the paper as incremental rather than gap-closing on item (1).

**This caveat is not resolved in this document and must not be presented to a journal as resolved.** It is the single highest-priority open item carried into `FINAL_RESEARCH_STATUS.md`.
